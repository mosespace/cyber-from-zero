It is often easier to trick a person than to break a computer. **Social engineering** uses trust, fear, curiosity and kindness against us.

## Common tricks

- **Pretexting**: inventing a story. "Hi, I am from IT, I need your password to fix your laptop."
- **Baiting**: leaving a USB stick labelled "Salaries" on a desk.
- **Tailgating**: following someone through a locked door.
- **Quid pro quo**: "I will help you if you do this one thing for me."

## Why it works

People want to be helpful, hate to look rude, and obey anyone who sounds important.

## How to defend

1. Verify who someone is using a number you already know.
2. Real IT teams never ask for your password.
3. It is OK to say, "Let me check and call you back."
4. Do not plug in unknown USB drives.

## Remember

- You are the last firewall.
- Politely checking is never rude.
